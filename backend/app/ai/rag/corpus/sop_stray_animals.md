# Animal Control — Stray Animal SOP

## Scope

STRAY_ANIMALS covers stray and free-roaming animals on public land: dog
packs exhibiting aggressive behaviour, cattle or livestock obstructing roads
or grazing on public grounds, suspected-rabid animal sightings, and requests
for sterilisation or vaccination drives in a ward with a rising stray
population.

Two cases go elsewhere. A dead animal on a public road or in a public space,
once the animal is no longer a live hazard, is SANITATION — carcass removal
and disposal is handled by sanitation crews with the appropriate equipment,
not by the animal capture team. A person who has already been bitten and
requires medical treatment is a HEALTH matter for post-exposure treatment
and reporting, even though Animal Control separately handles capturing the
animal responsible; the two teams coordinate but the medical response is not
Animal Control's function.

## Ownership

Animal Control owns this category. The escalation officer is the
department's zonal animal control officer, supported by ward-level capture
teams equipped for safe handling.

## Response norms

Critical within 4 hours, high within 24 hours, medium within 72 hours, low
within 168 hours. A suspected-rabid animal or an aggressive pack near a
school is critical; cattle blocking a busy road is high; a routine
sterilisation request for a stable stray population is medium; a general
population survey request is low.

## Typical materials and cost drivers

Capture nets and cages, tranquiliser kits for large or aggressive animals,
vaccination doses for anti-rabies drives, sterilisation surgical supplies,
and transport vehicles for relocation to a shelter. Cost is driven by the
number of animals involved in a single callout, by whether tranquilisation
requires a veterinarian on site, by transport distance to the nearest
shelter or veterinary facility, and by whether a ward requires a scheduled
mass sterilisation drive rather than a one-off capture.

## Escalation

The ward capture team responds first to individual reports. Unresolved past
50% of the window, it escalates to the zonal animal control officer; past
75%, to the district officer, who can authorise an additional capture team
from a neighbouring zone. Any suspected-rabid animal, an aggressive pack near
a school, or an incident already involving a bite escalates immediately to
the city-level Animal Control head office, which coordinates with the Health
Department on post-exposure response.
