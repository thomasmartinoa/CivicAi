# Electricity Board — Power Infrastructure SOP

## Scope

ELECTRICITY covers the public power distribution network: fallen or sagging
overhead lines, damaged or leaning poles, exposed or live wiring, faulty
street lighting, transformer faults including oil leaks or humming/burning
smells, and unscheduled outages affecting a street, block, or ward.

Two cases are handed elsewhere. A streetlight pole that is structurally
intact but has become an obstruction because it stands inside a footpath
being resurfaced is ROADS, since the fix is footpath realignment, not an
electrical repair. A complaint about noise or fumes from a private diesel
generator run by a resident or business, with no fault in the public grid
involved, is not ELECTRICITY at all and is redirected to civic enforcement
rather than the Electricity Board, since the Board has no jurisdiction over
private equipment.

## Ownership

Electricity Board owns this category citywide. The escalation officer is the
Board's duty engineer for the zone in which the fault is reported; each zone
maintains an on-call lineman roster for after-hours live-wire emergencies.

## Response norms

Critical within 4 hours, high within 24 hours, medium within 72 hours, low
within 168 hours. A live wire down in a public area, or a transformer fire
risk, is always critical regardless of time of day. A single faulty
streetlight with no safety exposure is typically low; a block-wide outage
affecting a hospital or school is high or critical depending on duration.

## Typical materials and cost drivers

LED street luminaires, galvanised steel or concrete poles, armoured
underground cable, aerial bunched cable for overhead runs, distribution
transformers, and fuse and circuit-breaker units. Cost is driven by whether
the fault is underground (excavation adds significantly to a cable repair)
or overhead, by the voltage class of the line affected, by whether a
transformer needs full replacement versus an oil top-up or gasket repair, and
by how many connections are downstream of the fault, since restoring a
transformer feeding an entire block costs more in coordinated switching time
than a single-house service drop.

## Escalation

The zonal duty engineer is first responder for any live-wire or outage
report. Unresolved past 50% of the response window, it escalates to the
regional supervisor; past 75%, to the district engineer, who can authorise
emergency contractor deployment outside normal shift hours. Any live-wire
report involving public safety, or an outage affecting a hospital, water
pumping station, or more than one ward, escalates immediately to the
city-level Electricity Board control room.
