# Flood Control Authority — Waterlogging and Drainage SOP

## Scope

FLOODING covers rainwater and waterlogging: blocked or silted storm drains,
waterlogged roads and underpasses after rain, overflowing lakes or retention
ponds during monsoon, and inadequate stormwater drain capacity causing
recurring flooding at the same low-lying spot. It also covers preventive
desilting requests ahead of the monsoon season.

Two cases go elsewhere. Sewage or foul-water overflow — recognisable by
smell and by manholes rather than storm-drain grates — is SEWAGE, even when
it visually resembles flooding, because the response requires a jetting crew
for the sewer line rather than desilting of a stormwater drain. A road
surface that has failed (potholed, sunk) after floodwater has receded, with
no standing water remaining, is ROADS, since the outstanding problem is
surface repair, not drainage.

## Ownership

Flood Control Authority owns this category. The escalation officer is the
Authority's zonal drainage engineer, who coordinates with the ward's storm
drain maintenance crew and, during active monsoon events, with the district
disaster management cell.

## Response norms

Critical within 4 hours, high within 24 hours, medium within 72 hours, low
within 168 hours. Waterlogging that has entered homes or cut off a road with
no alternate route is critical; a low-lying stretch flooding during heavy
rain but draining within hours is high or medium depending on recurrence;
routine pre-monsoon desilting requests are low.

## Typical materials and cost drivers

Desilting machinery hire, sandbags, submersible pumps for emergency
dewatering, RCC drain slab covers, geotextile lining for erosion-prone drain
banks, and warning barricades for flooded underpasses. Cost is driven by the
length and depth of drain requiring desilting, by whether pumping equipment
must be deployed and for how many hours, by the volume of silt or debris
removed (illegal dumping into drains is a major recurring cost driver), and
by whether a permanent capacity upgrade is warranted after repeated flooding
at the same spot rather than another season of temporary desilting.

## Escalation

The ward drainage crew responds first to a waterlogging report. Unresolved
past 50% of the window, it escalates to the zonal drainage engineer; past
75%, to the district engineer, who can authorise pump deployment from
another zone. Flooding that has entered homes, cut off an arterial road, or
affects more than one ward escalates immediately to the city-level Flood
Control Authority control room, which activates the district disaster
management cell during active monsoon emergencies.
