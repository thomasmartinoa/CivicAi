# Education Department — Government School Infrastructure SOP

## Scope

EDUCATION covers infrastructure at government schools and colleges: damaged
classroom furniture, leaking roofs, broken windows or doors, unsafe boundary
walls, insufficient or unusable toilet blocks, and missing or damaged
teaching equipment such as blackboards and benches reported by staff or
parents through the public complaint channel.

Two cases go elsewhere. A wiring fault, dead circuit, or damaged transformer
serving a school building is ELECTRICITY, not EDUCATION, since the fix
requires the Electricity Board's linemen rather than the department's own
maintenance staff — the school reports it, but routing still sends it to the
Board. A structural collapse risk, an illegal extension on school grounds, or
major civil work such as a new block foundation is CONSTRUCTION, since it
needs Public Works' engineering and enforcement capacity rather than routine
school upkeep.

## Ownership

Education Department owns this category. The escalation officer is the
department's zonal education officer, supported by the school's head teacher,
who is expected to file and track the initial complaint.

## Response norms

Critical within 4 hours, high within 24 hours, medium within 72 hours, low
within 168 hours. A collapsed ceiling section or an unsafe toilet block
during school hours is critical or high; a leaking roof during the dry
season with no immediate class disruption is medium; a broken bench or worn
paint is low.

## Typical materials and cost drivers

Classroom furniture (benches, desks, blackboards), roofing sheets and
waterproofing compound, door and window fittings, boundary wall repair
material, and sanitary fittings for toilet blocks. Cost is driven by the
number of classrooms affected, by whether roof repair requires scaffolding
and full re-sheeting versus a localised patch, by whether toilet block
repairs need to happen outside school hours to avoid disrupting classes, and
by how urgently furniture must be replaced ahead of an exam or admission
cycle.

## Escalation

The head teacher files and the ward education supervisor makes the first
visit. Unresolved past 50% of the window, it escalates to the zonal education
officer; past 75%, to the district education officer, who can authorise
emergency repair funds outside the normal maintenance budget cycle. A safety
hazard to students, or damage affecting more than one school in a ward,
escalates immediately to the city-level Education Department head office.
