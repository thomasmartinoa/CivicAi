# Sanitation Department — Sewage and Drainage SOP

## Scope

SEWAGE covers the underground foul-water network: open or missing manhole
covers, sewer line blockages and overflow, foul odour from a drain, and
cross-connections where sewage enters a stormwater or water supply line. It
also covers septic tank overflow complaints on public land in areas not yet
connected to the underground network.

Two cases go elsewhere. Rainwater pooling on a road or in a low-lying area,
with no foul smell or sewage involved, is FLOODING — the crew and equipment
(desilting of stormwater drains versus jetting of sewer lines) differ, so
intake distinguishes "clean water pooling" from "foul water overflow" before
routing. Solid waste dumped near or into a drain, where the primary complaint
is rubbish rather than liquid overflow, is SANITATION; a sewage crew clearing
a blockage will remove solid waste found in the line, but a complaint that is
purely about visible litter near a drain mouth is not escalated as SEWAGE.

## Ownership

Sanitation Department owns this category, through its underground drainage
wing. The escalation officer is the department's zonal drainage engineer,
supported by ward-level jetting machine operators.

## Response norms

Critical within 4 hours, high within 24 hours, medium within 72 hours, low
within 168 hours. An open manhole on a pedestrian route or a road, or sewage
overflow entering homes or a water source, is critical; a slow-draining line
with no overflow is medium; a missing but barricaded manhole cover pending
replacement is low.

## Typical materials and cost drivers

Cast-iron and reinforced-concrete manhole covers, PVC and RCC sewer pipe by
diameter, jetting machine hire for line clearance, sandbags and barricades
for overflow containment, and disinfectant for post-overflow cleanup. Cost is
driven by pipe diameter and depth (a trunk sewer repair is far costlier than
a lateral connection), by whether the blockage requires excavation to
access, by how far the overflow has spread and what it has contaminated, and
by whether the site needs repeat jetting due to a recurring root-intrusion or
grease blockage.

## Escalation

The ward jetting operator responds first to overflow and blockage reports.
Unresolved past 50% of the window, it escalates to the zonal drainage
engineer; past 75%, to the district engineer, who can authorise a second
jetting crew from another ward. Any overflow reaching a water source, a
school, or affecting more than one ward escalates immediately to the
city-level Sanitation Department head office, which coordinates with the
Health Department where contamination risk is present.
