# Water Supply Department — Potable Water SOP

## Scope

WATER covers the potable water supply network: burst or leaking supply
pipes, contaminated or discoloured tap water, low pressure or no-supply
complaints, damaged public stand-posts and tanker delivery points, and faulty
water meters. It also covers illegal tapping of the supply line that reduces
pressure for legitimate connections downstream.

Two cases go elsewhere. A pipe burst that has flooded a road with clean water
but the road surface itself is undamaged is still WATER for the leak repair,
but if the standing water has been sitting long enough to be reclassified as
a waterlogging hazard with no active leak found, it is FLOODING and handled
by drain clearance rather than pipe repair. A complaint about foul-smelling
water backing up from a drain or manhole, as opposed to the tap, is SEWAGE —
the two networks run in the same trenches in older wards and citizens
frequently conflate them, so intake confirms which water is at issue (tap
versus drain) before routing.

## Ownership

Water Supply Department owns this category. The escalation officer is the
department's zonal engineer, supported by a ward-level valve operator who can
authorise emergency shutoffs on a burst main.

## Response norms

Critical within 4 hours, high within 24 hours, medium within 72 hours, low
within 168 hours. A burst main flooding a road or entering homes is critical;
contaminated water reported near a school or clinic is high; a slow leak with
no immediate loss of supply is medium; a faulty meter reading is low.

## Typical materials and cost drivers

PVC and HDPE pipe by the metre in various diameters, ductile iron pipe for
trunk mains, brass and PVC valve fittings, water meters, chlorination
tablets for contamination response, and stand-post fittings. Cost is driven
by pipe diameter and material (trunk main repairs cost far more than a
household service line), by whether the repair requires road-cutting and
reinstatement, by how many connections are without supply while the repair
is underway (justifying tanker deployment as an interim measure), and by
whether contamination requires a wider network flush beyond the reported
point.

## Escalation

The ward valve operator responds first to isolate a leak or burst. Unresolved
past 50% of the window, it escalates to the zonal engineer; past 75%, to the
district engineer, who can authorise tanker supply to affected households.
Any contamination complaint near a school, hospital, or affecting more than
one ward escalates immediately to the city-level Water Supply Department
head office, which coordinates with the Health Department on public health
advisories.
