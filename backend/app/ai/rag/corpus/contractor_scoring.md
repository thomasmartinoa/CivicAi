# Contractor Scoring Policy

## Purpose

When a work order is routed, every contractor registered for the tenant is
scored and the highest-scoring one is assigned. The score is a simple
weighted sum of four factors, deliberately explicit rather than tuned by a
model, so a routing justification can cite exactly which factor decided the
assignment. Specialisation match dominates the score, track record and
current availability matter next, and locality is only a tie-breaker.

## Specialisation match — weight 40

A contractor whose registered specialisations include the complaint's
category receives 40 points, the single largest factor in the score. This
reflects the policy that a contractor who does not do this kind of work
should almost never outrank one who does, regardless of rating or
availability — a highly-rated general contractor with no roads
specialisation should not be routed a pothole repair ahead of a mediocre
roads specialist.

## Track record — rating × 6

A contractor's rating, on a 0-to-5 scale, is multiplied by 6, contributing up
to 30 points at a perfect rating. This is deliberately smaller than the
specialisation weight: a contractor's history of good work should influence
which qualified contractor gets the job, but should not by itself overcome a
lack of relevant specialisation.

## Availability — up to 20, minus 2 per active job

Every contractor starts with a workload allowance of 20 points, reduced by 2
points for every job currently active on their workload, floored at zero
rather than going negative. A contractor with no active jobs keeps the full
20 points; one with 10 or more active jobs contributes nothing from this
factor. This keeps routing from repeatedly overloading the single top-rated,
best-specialised contractor in a category while others sit idle.

## Locality — bonus of 10

If the contractor's registered zone matches the complaint's district
(case-insensitively), a flat 10-point bonus is added. This is a tie-breaker,
not a primary factor: it can separate two similarly qualified and available
contractors, but it is not large enough to override a real specialisation or
availability gap.

## How the score is used

The four contributions are summed and contractors are ranked highest first;
the top-ranked contractor is assigned and the runner-up is not automatically
notified. When two contractors are close in score, the routing justification
should note which factor — specialisation, rating, availability, or zone —
produced the separation, since that is the information an escalation officer
needs when a citizen or contractor questions the assignment.
