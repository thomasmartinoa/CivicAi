# Health Department — Public Health Hazard SOP

## Scope

HEALTH covers public health hazards on public land and at public health
facilities: mosquito breeding sites in standing water on public property,
unhygienic conditions at a public health centre, improper disposal of
biomedical waste, food safety complaints against street vendors on public
land, and outbreak-pattern reports such as a cluster of illness traced to a
specific public source.

Two cases go elsewhere. Ordinary household or street garbage accumulation,
even when a citizen describes it as "a health hazard," is SANITATION unless
it is specifically biomedical or hazardous waste — general solid waste is
handled by collection trucks, not by the Health Department's inspection
staff. Contaminated tap water reported at the source (a discoloured or
foul-tasting municipal supply) is WATER, since the fix is a network flush or
pipe repair; the Health Department is looped in for advisories but does not
own the repair.

## Ownership

Health Department owns this category. The escalation officer is the
department's zonal public health inspector, supported by ward-level health
workers who conduct routine inspections and fogging operations.

## Response norms

Critical within 4 hours, high within 24 hours, medium within 72 hours, low
within 168 hours. A confirmed disease cluster or biomedical waste exposure
risk is critical; a mosquito breeding site near a school is high; a routine
food-safety inspection request is medium; a minor hygiene complaint at a
low-footfall facility is low.

## Typical materials and cost drivers

Larvicide and fogging chemicals, biomedical waste disposal contracts,
disinfectant for public facility cleaning, personal protective equipment for
inspection staff, and diagnostic test kits for outbreak investigation. Cost
is driven by the area requiring fogging or larvicide treatment, by whether a
biomedical waste incident requires specialised hazardous-waste contractors
rather than routine sanitation crews, by the number of facilities inspected
in an outbreak investigation, and by whether public advisories require
translated multi-language print and broadcast material.

## Escalation

The ward health worker responds first to routine hazard reports. Unresolved
past 50% of the window, it escalates to the zonal public health inspector;
past 75%, to the district health officer, who can authorise an expanded
fogging or inspection operation. Any confirmed disease cluster or biomedical
waste exposure escalates immediately to the city-level Health Department
head office, which coordinates with state health authorities where required.
