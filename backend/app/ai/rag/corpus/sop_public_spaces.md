# Parks & Recreation — Public Spaces SOP

## Scope

PUBLIC_SPACES covers parks, playgrounds, public gardens, community grounds,
and street furniture such as benches and public monuments: damaged play
equipment, overgrown or unmaintained park vegetation, broken boundary
fencing, vandalised benches or monuments, and unsafe or locked public toilets
within a park or garden.

Two cases go elsewhere. A non-functioning light pole inside a park is
ELECTRICITY, not PUBLIC_SPACES, since the fault is in the shared power
network that Parks & Recreation does not maintain itself — the department
files the request but the Electricity Board's crew does the repair. An
encroachment where a park boundary has been built over, or an unauthorised
structure erected on park land, is CONSTRUCTION, since it requires
enforcement and demolition authority that sits with Public Works, not
routine park maintenance.

## Ownership

Parks & Recreation owns this category. The escalation officer is the
department's zonal parks officer, supported by ward-level gardeners and
groundskeepers who handle day-to-day upkeep requests.

## Response norms

Critical within 4 hours, high within 24 hours, medium within 72 hours, low
within 168 hours. A collapsed or sharp-edged play structure that could injure
a child is critical or high; an unlocked but non-hazardous broken gate is
medium; overgrown grass or a chipped bench is low.

## Typical materials and cost drivers

Playground equipment parts (swings, slides, safety matting), chain-link and
ornamental fencing, paving stones for walkways, park benches, turf and
sapling stock for landscaping, and irrigation fittings. Cost is driven by
whether play equipment needs full replacement versus a spare-part repair, by
the area of vegetation requiring clearance, by whether vandalism repairs
require security measures (CCTV, fencing) to prevent recurrence, and by
seasonal demand for landscaping labour around planting season.

## Escalation

The ward groundskeeper responds first to routine maintenance requests.
Unresolved past 50% of the window, it escalates to the zonal parks officer;
past 75%, to the district parks officer, who can authorise contractor
deployment for larger repairs. A safety hazard involving children, or damage
affecting a park serving more than one ward, escalates immediately to the
city-level Parks & Recreation head office.
