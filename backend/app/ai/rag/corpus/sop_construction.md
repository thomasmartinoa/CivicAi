# Public Works Department — Construction and Excavation SOP

## Scope

CONSTRUCTION covers building work and its side effects on public land:
illegal or unpermitted construction encroaching on a road, footpath, or
setback; excavation dug for any purpose (utility laying, foundation work,
survey pits) and left open, unfilled, or badly reinstated; unsafe scaffolding
or building material stored on a public right of way; and structural hazards
from an under-construction or abandoned building adjoining a public street.

Two cases go elsewhere. A surface crack, pothole, or sunken patch on a road
that is otherwise intact, with no dig or building work associated with it, is
ROADS — Public Works' resurfacing crews handle that, not the enforcement side
that deals with excavation and illegal structures. A complaint about waste or
debris dumped at a construction site with no excavation, encroachment, or
open trench involved is SANITATION, since the issue is solid waste
accumulation rather than an unsafe or unauthorised dig or build.

## Ownership

Public Works Department owns this category, through its enforcement and
engineering wing. The head officer for Public Works is the escalation
contact; the ward assistant engineer performs the first site inspection to
confirm whether a structure or excavation is permitted before any notice is
issued.

## Response norms

Critical within 4 hours, high within 24 hours, medium within 72 hours, low
within 168 hours. An open, unbarricaded trench on a pedestrian route or an
unstable structure leaning over a footpath is critical or high; a minor
unpermitted extension with no immediate safety risk is typically medium or
low, pending a scheduled enforcement visit.

## Typical materials and cost drivers

Barricading and warning signage for open excavations, quick-set concrete or
compacted fill for emergency trench closure, demolition equipment hire for
unsafe structures, and legal notice printing and service costs for
enforcement action. Cost is driven by the size and depth of the excavation,
whether the site requires round-the-clock security until reinstated, whether
demolition of an illegal structure needs heavy machinery access, and whether
the responsible utility or builder can be identified and billed rather than
the department absorbing the reinstatement cost itself.

## Escalation

The ward assistant engineer inspects first and issues a notice if warranted.
Unresolved past 50% of the response window, it escalates to the block
supervisor; past 75%, to the district engineer, who can authorise emergency
closure of an open trench at department cost pending recovery from the
responsible party. A structure at risk of collapse, or an excavation on a
road carrying traffic from more than one ward, escalates immediately to the
city-level Public Works head office and, where a collapse risk to life
exists, to the Fire Department in parallel.
