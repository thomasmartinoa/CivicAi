# Sanitation Department — Solid Waste SOP

## Scope

SANITATION covers solid waste: overflowing or missing public bins, missed
household or street collection rounds, illegal dumping of garbage or
construction debris on public land, uncleared street sweeping, and dead
animal removal from public roads. It also covers segregation compliance
complaints at community collection points.

Two cases go elsewhere. Liquid waste — sewage overflow, a blocked drain
discharging foul water, or an open manhole — is SEWAGE, not SANITATION, even
though both are handled by departments that share the same seeded name;
routing still records the distinct category so response crews (solid-waste
trucks versus jetting machines) are dispatched correctly. Waste that has
accumulated specifically because a construction site is inactive and
unsecured, where the underlying complaint is the unsafe or abandoned site
rather than the rubbish itself, is CONSTRUCTION.

## Ownership

Sanitation Department owns this category. The escalation officer is the
department's zonal sanitary inspector, supported by ward-level collection
supervisors who track truck routes and bin schedules.

## Response norms

Critical within 4 hours, high within 24 hours, medium within 72 hours, low
within 168 hours. A large dumping pile blocking a road or drain inlet is high
or critical; a single overflowing bin is typically medium; a missed sweeping
round with no immediate hazard is low.

## Typical materials and cost drivers

Compactor truck trips, replacement bins in various sizes, PPE for collection
staff handling hazardous or bulk waste, bleaching powder and disinfectant for
dead animal sites, and signage for designated collection points. Cost is
driven by the volume and type of waste (construction debris and bulk waste
cost more per trip than household waste), by whether illegal dumping requires
repeat enforcement visits to the same site, by distance to the nearest
transfer station, and by whether a site needs disinfection after clearance.

## Escalation

The ward collection supervisor is first responder for missed rounds and
overflowing bins. Unresolved past 50% of the window, it escalates to the
zonal sanitary inspector; past 75%, to the district sanitation officer, who
can reassign trucks from another ward. Repeat illegal dumping at the same
site, or a health-hazard-level accumulation near a school or water source,
escalates immediately to the city-level Sanitation Department head office,
which can involve enforcement for penalty action against repeat offenders.
