# Fire Department — Fire Hazard SOP

## Scope

FIRE_HAZARD covers conditions that create a risk of fire or that obstruct
fire response on public or quasi-public property: exposed or arcing
electrical wiring near flammable material, illegal storage of combustible
goods (LPG cylinders, firecrackers, scrap) in residential or commercial
areas, blocked fire lanes or hydrant access, and buildings operating without
required fire safety clearances such as extinguishers or emergency exits.

Two cases go elsewhere. A damaged or sparking electrical line with no
associated flammable material nearby and no fire risk beyond the fault
itself is ELECTRICITY — the Electricity Board repairs the line, and only
escalates to Fire if the fault has already ignited something. An
unauthorised structure blocking a designated fire lane is CONSTRUCTION for
the demolition or removal order, though the Fire Department is notified in
parallel given the access risk and jointly signs off once the lane is
cleared.

## Ownership

Fire Department owns this category. The escalation officer is the
department's zonal fire safety officer, supported by the station officer at
the nearest fire station, who dispatches a safety inspection team.

## Response norms

Critical within 4 hours, high within 24 hours, medium within 72 hours, low
within 168 hours. An active fire risk (sparking wire near stored fuel, a
blocked hydrant during an emergency) is always critical; illegal storage of
combustibles in a densely populated area is high; a building missing
extinguisher certification with no immediate ignition source is medium; a
minor signage or exit-marking deficiency is low.

## Typical materials and cost drivers

Fire extinguishers and refill cylinders, emergency exit signage, fire-rated
door hardware, hydrant valve replacement parts, and barricading to keep fire
lanes clear. Cost is driven by the scale of the hazard (a single building
inspection versus a market-area sweep), by whether illegal storage requires
supervised safe removal of hazardous material, by the number of hydrants or
lanes needing clearance, and by whether repeat non-compliance triggers a
formal enforcement notice with legal costs.

## Escalation

The nearest fire station's inspection team responds first. Unresolved past
50% of the window, it escalates to the zonal fire safety officer; past 75%,
to the district fire officer, who can authorise a joint inspection with
Public Works or Electricity Board where the hazard spans categories. Any
active fire risk, or a hazard affecting a school, hospital, or market
serving more than one ward, escalates immediately to the city-level Fire
Department control room.
